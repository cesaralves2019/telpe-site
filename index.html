Today

Hundreds of public websites, each with its own menu. The concept puts one question in front of all of them.

The State already publishes almost everything people need. The work is finding it. A front door that starts from the question, not from the department, is the whole idea; the sites behind it stay exactly where they are.

Ask. Get an answer that cites its source. Then do it, here.

1

Ask in your own words

English or Irish, typed or spoken. No account, no menu to learn. If the question needs it, the concept asks one thing back: household size, county, whether you’re self-employed.

2

Read an answer you can trust

Built only from what public bodies publish, with each sentence linked to its source page. When the State’s page changes, the answer changes.

3

Finish the taskconcept · 2027

Sign in with MyGovID only when a task needs it. Renew a passport, pay motor tax, update an address across bodies, and track it in one place.

Private by design.

Share only what’s needed

The concept asks for your county, not your Eircode, unless an office lookup needs it.

Nothing is kept

Prompts and answers are not stored; a short cache keyed by a hash of the question, never the text, expires within two hours.

No ads, no tracking

No advertising, no third-party trackers, no profile built from your questions.

Public sources only

Answers are drawn from what public bodies publish. The model does not invent eligibility rules or deadlines; when it does not know, it says so and points to a person.

If this were built.

NowAnswers with sources across the main public bodies. English and Irish. No account.
Within a yearSign in with MyGovID. Passport renewal, motor tax, Register of Electors and address changes completed inside the front door, with status tracking.
LaterBenefits applications and appointments (HSE, NDLS, Intreo) started here and handed to the body that decides. Every public website serving more than 100,000 people a year plugged in.

What this is. A design mock-up by Telpe AI, Dublin, made in October 2026 after the launch of America.gov, to imagine the same idea for Ireland. It is not a government website and is not affiliated with the Government of Ireland, gov.ie or any public body. The names of public bodies appear only to make the concept legible. Nothing is collected; the answers are scripted examples and are not advice.
